# Movement sequences

The ordered actions below follow the source videos. The demonstrations teach grip and body technique; the separate 3D studies explain prop geometry and static spatial landmarks.

Positions and actions are source-derived. Diagram distances and depth offsets are illustrative. The original video supplies thumb/index support changes, thumb/pinky passages inside or outside the elbow, shoulder clearance, and body turns. The site does not animate a reconstructed person.

## Continuous movement checkpoints

These checkpoints are observations to compare with the demonstration, not generated joint poses. Thumb-led and pinky-led antispin use different elbow passages. A future body reconstruction must match those passages and the support changes throughout the full movement, not merely clear a set of collision checks.

### grip

1. **Center.** Support the center between the thumb pad and index finger; relax the other fingers. Source: [1:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=73s).
2. **Index support.** Open the grip so the shaft rests along the index finger. Source: [1:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=110s).
3. **Web support.** Let support shift to the thumb/index web; do not wind the wrist around the staff. Source: [2:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=140s).

### isolation

1. **Low.** Aim the selected end at the fixed focal point. Source: [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sGrt1Uj8Ud4&t=12s).
2. **Right.** Carry the grip to the right without moving the selected endpoint. Source: [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sGrt1Uj8Ud4&t=12s).
3. **High.** Continue around the same point; accommodate the thumb-joint support. Source: [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sGrt1Uj8Ud4&t=12s).
4. **Left.** Return down the opposite side while the selected end stays anchored. Source: [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sGrt1Uj8Ud4&t=12s).

### extension

1. **Low.** Reach outward with the thumb end pointing away from the center. Source: [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9DO8BIlXrs&t=12s).
2. **Right.** Carry the extended hand and staff together to the side. Source: [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9DO8BIlXrs&t=12s).
3. **High.** Continue overhead without adding a separate wrist spin. Source: [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9DO8BIlXrs&t=12s).
4. **Left.** Complete the large circle with the arm carrying the staff. Source: [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9DO8BIlXrs&t=12s).

### antispin

### inspin

## Every lesson section, in teaching order

Timing variants retain the source's hand phase and direction. Wheel-plane demonstrations keep distinct left and right working planes. Front-alignment antibrid studies preserve projected relationships while separating the solid staves in depth. Still poses and spatial references do not autoplay.

### beginner-01: Grip, timing & direction

#### 1. Find the balance point

Source: [0:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=44s)–1:13.

Grip: Place thumb and index finger at the middle mark.

Stance: Hold one staff in front of you while locating its center.

Focus: Equal length on each side of the hand.

1. **Locate.** Find the center mark, then check that the two sides balance.
2. **Hold.** Keep the grip centered as you begin turning the staff.

Representation: source video.

#### 2. Thumb–index pinch

Source: [1:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=73s)–1:50.

Grip: Thumb pad against index finger; other fingers remain available rather than clamped.

Stance: Keep your hand where you can watch the contact point.

Focus: Let the shaft rotate without squeezing it in a closed fist.

1. **Pinch.** Support the balance point between thumb and index finger.
2. **Release tension.** Let the remaining fingers move away while preserving control.

Representation: source video.

#### 3. Open the grip in both directions

Source: [1:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=110s)–3:29.

Grip: Allow the contact surface to change as the staff rolls through the hand.

Stance: Try one hand, then both, with space around your fingertips.

Focus: Follow the grip change instead of forcing a wrist twist.

1. **Index support.** Turn until the shaft lies along the extended index finger, with the thumb lightly supporting its side.
2. **Web support.** Turn the other way into the open-hand position, supporting the shaft in the thumb–index web.
3. **Reverse.** Move gently back and forth through both positions, then try the other hand.

Representation: source video.

#### 4. Together time · same direction

Source: [3:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=217s)–4:35.

Grip: Keep a consistent thumb-end reference on both staves.

Stance: Give both hands room to trace matching circles.

Focus: The marked ends reach each part of their circles together.

1. **Match.** Start the marked ends at the same point in the cycle.
2. **Travel.** Move both clockwise, or both counterclockwise, maintaining that relationship.

Representation: extension.

#### 5. Split time · same direction

Source: [4:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=275s)–4:45.

Grip: Continue tracking the same end of each staff.

Stance: Let the hands occupy opposite sides of their circular paths.

Focus: When one marked end is high, the other is low.

1. **Separate.** Place the marked ends half a cycle apart.
2. **Match speed.** Move in the same direction without closing that gap.

Representation: extension.

#### 6. Together time · opposite directions

Source: [4:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=285s)–5:01.

Grip: Use the marked ends to identify the meeting points.

Stance: Mirror the two arm movements across your centerline.

Focus: Top and bottom are together; left and right are apart.

1. **Meet high.** Bring the marked ends toward the top from opposite sides.
2. **Open.** Move apart to the sides and meet again at the bottom.

Representation: extension.

#### 7. Split time · opposite directions

Source: [5:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=301s)–5:13.

Grip: Keep the same marked-end reference.

Stance: Start with one hand high and the other low.

Focus: The two side positions are the shared points.

1. **Separate vertically.** Place one marked end high and the other low.
2. **Meet sideways.** Move in opposite directions toward the same side, then continue around.

Representation: extension.

#### 8. Link the four timing modes

Source: [5:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqJoUgprXws&t=313s)–7:14.

Grip: Maintain the reference ends as you change direction.

Stance: Keep the pattern small enough to pause clearly at each shared point.

Focus: Change direction at a matching position, rather than jumping a hand across the circle.

1. **Together same → together opposite.** Reverse one hand when both reference ends meet at the top or bottom.
2. **Together opposite → split same.** Reverse one hand when the ends are separated across the sides.
3. **Split same → split opposite.** Reverse at the high–low alignment.
4. **Split opposite → together same.** Reverse at a shared side position.

Representation: transition. A shared-position timing study. Reverse one hand at a meeting or opposed position; the source shows the entry and grip changes.

### beginner-02: Hip & shoulder reels

#### 1. Single-staff hip reel

Source: [0:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7nPzvxhRrpI&t=48s)–1:19.

Grip: Let the centered grip open as the pinky end goes behind you.

Stance: Work beside the hip, keeping the elbow available for the rear passage.

Focus: Pinky behind at the waist; thumb in front.

1. **Thumb up.** Begin with the thumb end up and the pinky end down.
2. **Pinky behind.** Send the pinky end behind the back as the thumb end extends to the side.
3. **Pass the elbow.** Let the pinky end pass behind the elbow, then bring the thumb end across the front of the waist.
4. **Return.** Arrive thumb-up again and connect the positions smoothly.

### beginner-05: Antispin
